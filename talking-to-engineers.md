# Talking to Engineers

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. Not yet reviewed.

**Purpose.** How to not get stonewalled, by people whose stonewalling is a safety control.

## Why They Are Skeptical and Why They Are Right

The controls engineer has run that system without an incident since before your company had a security team, and their skepticism of you is not personality, it is pattern recognition. IT has pushed agents that crashed HMIs, scans that [took lines down](what-not-to-scan.md), patches that voided support, and initiatives that arrived loudly and left quietly. From the plant floor, corporate security programs look like weather: they blow through, break something, and change with the CISO. You are not owed trust here. You are offered the chance to be the exception, which is more than fair.

## Ask Before You Recommend

Your first month is questions, not findings, and the questions are the credential. What does this process make, what happens when it stops, what almost went wrong last year, what did the last IT initiative break. Write down the answers visibly, it signals the answers matter. The moment you catch yourself starting a sentence with "you should," in month one, stop the sentence. Recommendations arrive in month two, wearing the plant's own priorities, or they arrive dead.

## Learn the Physical Process

Not the network diagram, the thing itself: what gets made, moved, heated, cleaned, or treated, and what the operators watch when they are worried. Walk the floor with someone who loves the place, most veterans do, and let them show off. An hour of genuine curiosity about the process buys more cooperation than a quarter of meetings, because it proves the thing they doubt most: that you understand there is a physical world on the other side of the packets, and that it outranks them.

## Never Touch Anything Without Agreement

Not a scan, not a login, not an unplugged cable, not once. The single time you touch something without the operator's yes will define the relationship permanently, and the story will outlast your tenure, plants have long memories and good storytelling. Put the rule in writing with a standing veto, then keep it with a lawyer's discipline. This costs you nothing, speed was never really on the table anyway, and it purchases the thing everything else depends on.

## Bring Something Useful Early

Show up with a gift before you show up with a finding. Passive visibility they did not have, a map of connections nobody had drawn, a vendor support conversation you handled, spare cycles fixing the corporate-side thing that has annoyed them for years. The gift proves you subtract work rather than add it, and it converts your arrival from an audit into an alliance. Every plant has a small backlog of things IT could fix in a week; ask for the list and pick one.

## Language That Lands and Language That Does Not

Lands: consequence, downtime, safe state, what would this mean for the line, help me understand, what do you need from corporate. Does not land: compliance gap, risk score, best practice, industry standard, mandatory, and above all the word "just," which prices your ignorance in one syllable. Translate everything into their currency, production protected and hassle removed, and cite [the priority order](the-inversion.md) as yours, not as a concession. When you must bring a hard requirement, bring the reason and the flexibility on method, engineers negotiate means all day, it is ends imposed without reasons that get you the wall.
